Types of RV trailers

Travel trailers

Conventional towable campers attach to a compatible receiver and hitch system. Layouts range from compact units to larger family floor plans.

Renting or buying a trailer?

Renting can help you learn which floor plan and campsite setup suit your group. Compare pickup and delivery options through our Michigan RV rental guide. When evaluating a used trailer, look beyond interior appearance: roof seams, floor softness, slide operation, frame condition and appliance function matter. Ask for maintenance records and arrange an independent inspection before committing. MRR does not list trailers for sale or provide dealer inventory.

Match the trailer to the tow vehicle

Use vehicle and trailer manufacturer documentation and actual loaded weights. Check gross vehicle and axle ratings, payload, tongue or pin weight, hitch ratings and braking equipment. A higher-rated component cannot raise the vehicle's own limits. Confirm compatibility with qualified professionals before towing, especially after changes to suspension, tires, hitch or cargo equipment.

Replacement parts and maintenance

Record trailer and component identification before ordering. Year and model alone may not identify the awning, converter, water heater or brake hardware. See RV parts and accessories and the etrailer towing guide. For insurance questions, see travel trailer insurance resources.

Inspect before a trip

  • Review tire, wheel and brake service history with a qualified technician.
  • Inspect visible roof and exterior seals for evidence of water intrusion.
  • Verify hitch, coupler, safety equipment and lighting are appropriate.
  • Confirm the campsite accommodates trailer length and utilities.
