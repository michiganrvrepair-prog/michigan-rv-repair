Used camper inspection checklist

Use this checklist to prepare for an independent pre-purchase inspection; it is not a certification of condition.

Before viewing the camper

Ask for the model year, floor plan, ownership documentation, maintenance records and any history of water intrusion, collision or major repairs. Confirm the seller permits an independent inspection and demonstration of the installed systems. Do not rely on photographs or a seller's verbal assurances alone.

Exterior and interior condition

Look for visible damage, staining, unusual odors, soft flooring and signs of previous repairs. Check accessible seams, windows, doors and storage compartments. A clean interior does not rule out hidden moisture or structural damage. A qualified inspector can determine whether further evaluation is appropriate.

System demonstrations

Ask the seller to demonstrate installed appliances and water, electrical and slide-out systems using the manufacturer's instructions. Stop if there is a suspected gas leak, electrical fault or binding mechanism. Do not open energized equipment or climb onto an unapproved roof. Record which systems could not be demonstrated and why.

Towing and ownership costs

Confirm the trailer's ratings and actual loaded weight against the tow vehicle and hitch system. Ask about tire and brake service records, storage, registration and anticipated repairs. Compare the purchase price with a realistic repair and maintenance budget. Consult a licensed provider about insurance questions.

  • Request a written inspection scope and report.
  • Verify which systems were tested and which were not accessible.
  • Resolve ownership and financing questions before paying.
  • Obtain separate estimates for significant defects.
