Start with the component, not just the RV model

Two campers of the same model year can have different appliances or hardware. Photograph the existing component and its data plate, and record the manufacturer, model, serial number and original part number. For a replacement, compare the dimensions, mounting pattern, electrical rating, connector type and applicable manufacturer compatibility information. Do not rely on a similar-looking photo alone.

What kind of RV part are you looking for?

Electrical

Identify system voltage, current rating, connector configuration and original equipment specifications before replacing a converter, fuse or breaker.

Plumbing

Confirm fitting type, diameter, pump model and potable-water compatibility. A leak may originate upstream of the visible drip.

Roof, awning and appliances

Check roof material, sealant compatibility, awning model and appliance data plates. Verify approved replacement parts and instructions.

Before ordering from a parts retailer

Check the seller's current stock, shipping estimate, return conditions and warranty. Some electrical or special-order components may have different return restrictions. A symptom does not prove a component is faulty: a refrigerator that is not cooling or a furnace that will not ignite can have several causes. When uncertain, arrange a diagnosis before ordering expensive parts.

For propane, mains electrical, structural or safety-critical components, use manufacturer-approved parts and qualified service rather than improvising a substitute. MRR does not certify a product or guarantee that a retailer's listing fits your RV.
