Understand the system before ordering

Many RVs use both nominal 12-volt DC and 120-volt AC systems. Converters, inverters, chargers and distribution panels serve different functions, although some equipment combines functions. Identify which system is affected and record the equipment model. A similar-looking part may have a different input voltage, output rating, battery charging profile or wiring configuration.

Converters, chargers and batteries

For a converter or charger, collect input and output specifications, battery chemistry compatibility and mounting details. For batteries, check manufacturer requirements, dimensions, terminal configuration and charging equipment compatibility. Do not assume a lithium battery can be substituted without reviewing the complete charging and protection system.

Fuses, breakers and connectors

Identify the original protective device type, voltage rating, current rating and approved application. Never increase a fuse or breaker rating to stop repeated trips; the underlying fault must be diagnosed. For connectors and cables, verify specified current capacity, conductor requirements and connector configuration. Mains electrical work and high-current battery systems require appropriate qualifications and precautions.

Information to collect

  • Equipment model and serial number, original part number and label photo.
  • System voltage, ratings and manufacturer compatibility documentation.
  • Dimensions and connector configuration, without exposing energized components.
  • Symptoms, recent repairs and whether shore power, battery power or both are affected.
